== Session 1: Noticing Your Inner Voice

[.chapter-quote,quote]
____
You cannot defeat your gremlin by fighting it. But you can notice it.
____

[.concept-box]
.Core Concepts
====
- *Inner Talk*: The thoughts that arise automatically in our minds
- *The Inner Gremlin*: The critical, negative voice within
- *The Space Between Stimulus and Response*: The zone where the freedom to choose lives
====

'''

=== Minsu's Day

Minsu, a five-year software developer, had a company-wide tech talk scheduled for three o'clock that afternoon. He was about to present the caching system he had spent three months building to thirty of his colleagues.

==== Morning Commute (9:00 AM)

Subway line 7. Minsu was doing one last review of his slides on his phone when a Slack notification popped up.

"Minsu, I'm really looking forward to your talk today! 😊"

It was Sujin from the backend team. Minsu smiled briefly — then his expression went flat. A voice rose inside his head.

[.quote-box]
____
_"Remember how nervous you were at last year's talk? Remember what your team lead said? This time will be exactly the same. Actually, it'll be worse."_
____

Minsu sighed. This voice. It had always been there. It appeared without fail at every important moment — at his graduation presentation, during job interviews, at his first project demo. *Repetitive. Automatic.*

_"Everyone saw your voice shake. You were so embarrassed. And you will be again today."_

Minsu pocketed his phone and closed his eyes. He kept telling himself to relax, but his heart was already pounding.

==== The Slide Spiral (11:00 AM)

Back at his desk, Minsu opened his slides again. Page 17 — a performance comparison graph caught his eye.

_"This graph... isn't it too simple? Team lead is going to look at this and say, 'That's all you've got?'"_

Minsu started tweaking the graph. He changed the colors, added a legend, adjusted the layout. Twenty minutes passed.

_"No, that's not right either. Was the original better?"_

He reverted everything. Then changed it again.

_"If you present this unprepared, everyone's going to wonder what you've been doing for three months."_

[TIP]
====
Perfectionism is often a trap created by the inner gremlin. It keeps you endlessly revising so you never get to the real work.
====

Minsu kept tweaking the slide — changing, reverting, changing again. After forty minutes, the graph looked exactly the same as it had at the start. He had lost forty minutes.

He closed his laptop and buried his face in his hands. _Why do I always do this?_

==== The Moment of Noticing (2:20 PM)

Minsu sat at his desk, staring blankly at his monitor. Less than an hour to go.

Then, unexpectedly, he recalled a piece he had stumbled across online the week before — a blog post about a book called _Taming Your Gremlin_.

[.quote-box]
____
The one thing a gremlin hates most is simply being noticed. The harder you fight it, the stronger it grows. But the moment you simply notice that it's there, its power begins to fade.
____

Minsu closed his eyes. This time, unlike before, he decided to actually listen to the voice in his head. *Without fighting it.* Just... observing it.

_"You're going to fail again. You always do."_

_'This voice really loves the word "always." It loves "never" and "everyone" too.'_

_"You're going to disappoint everyone."_

_'Everyone? All thirty people? Every single one?'_

_"When they see your hands shaking, how humiliating is that going to be..."_

_'Shaking hands... is that really such a big deal? Is it actually forbidden to be nervous?'_

Minsu breathed slowly. The voice was still there. But strangely, it felt different from before. It was as if someone had turned down the volume on a radio.

'''

=== Core Concept: Inner Talk

We reportedly have somewhere between 50,000 and 70,000 thoughts per day. A significant portion of those are automatic, repetitive, and often negative.

This *inner voice (Inner Talk)*:

- Arises automatically
- Feels like fact
- Follows repetitive patterns
- Influences our emotions and behavior

[.concept-box]
.The Inner Gremlin
====
The *Gremlin* is the critical, negative voice inside us.

Characteristics:

- *Extreme language*: "always," "never," "everyone," "no one"
- *Future prediction*: "You're going to...," "This will..."
- *Overgeneralization from the past*: "You always do this"
- *Speaking for others*: "People are going to think..."
====

==== Why Shouldn't You Fight the Gremlin?

When Minsu kept telling himself not to be nervous, his nervousness only intensified. Why?

[TIP]
====
What happens when someone tells you, "Don't think about a pink elephant"? Paradoxically, you think about it even more. Suppression produces the opposite effect.
====

*Fighting gives the gremlin energy.*

- The more you try to suppress it, the stronger it grows
- The more you try to argue back, the more evidence it produces
- The more you try to ignore it, the louder it shouts

So what should you do instead?

=== Awareness: The Space Between Stimulus and Response

Viktor Frankl wrote:

[.quote-box]
____
Between stimulus and response there is a space. In that space is our power to choose our response. In our response lies our growth and our freedom.

_— Viktor Frankl_
____

This is exactly what Minsu experienced. Between the inner voice (stimulus) and our emotions and actions (response), there is a *space*.

[cols="1,1,1", frame=all, grid=all]
|===
| *Stimulus* +
Inner voice
| → *The Space of Choice* +
Awareness
| *Response* +
Conscious choice
|===

In that space, we can:

1. *Notice* — "Ah, I hear that voice"
2. *Observe* — "There's that word 'always' again"
3. *Choose* — "Do I want to believe this thought or not?"

[.concept-box]
.The Power of Awareness
====
Awareness transforms automatic reactions into conscious choices:

*Before:* Inner voice → automatic reaction → emotion/behavior

*After:* Inner voice → *awareness* → conscious choice
====

==== The Three Stages of Awareness

1. *Recognition*
   - "I can hear my inner voice right now"
   - "This is a thought"

2. *Observation*
   - Observing without fighting
   - Looking at the pattern with curiosity

3. *Choice*
   - "Do I want to believe this thought?"
   - "Do I want to act on this thought?"

'''

=== Practice Exercises

==== Exercise 1: Catching Your Inner Voice

Over the next week, observe your inner voice once a day.

[.practice-box]
.Observation Journal
====
*Situation:* What happened?

*Inner voice:* What thoughts came up? (Write them down exactly.)

*Gremlin characteristics:* What patterns do you notice?

- [ ] Extreme language ("always," "never," etc.)
- [ ] Future prediction
- [ ] Overgeneralization from the past
- [ ] Speaking for others

*Physical sensations:* What did you feel in your body?
====

[TIP]
====
It may feel difficult at first. Try spending just five minutes at the end of each day to write it down. After a few days, patterns will start to emerge.
====

==== Exercise 2: Labeling

When you hear your inner voice, try placing a label on it:

- "Ah, a *judgment* is arising"
- "Ah, there's that *overgeneralization* again"
- "Ah, I hear *future worry* coming in"
- "Ah, *my gremlin* is talking"

Even this small act allows you to step back from the automatic reaction. Labeling is *the first step in separating yourself from your thoughts*.

==== Exercise 3: Naming Your Gremlin

Here is a fun approach: give your gremlin a name.

For example: "Perfecta," "Worrywart," "Comparator"

Giving it a name creates distance.

_"Perfecta is showing up again. Hey, Perfecta. There you are."_

'''

=== After Minsu's Talk

At three o'clock, Minsu began his presentation.

His voice trembled a little in the first few minutes. His hands shook too. But this time, something was different.

_"See? You're shaking. Everyone can tell."_

_'Ah, the gremlin is talking.'_

Minsu paused to catch his breath, then kept going. He did not try to make the trembling stop. He just... presented while trembling.

After fifteen minutes, Minsu noticed that he was actually focused on the content. He was explaining the caching system's architecture, sharing the performance improvements he had made.

When the presentation ended, several questions came in. Good questions. Minsu was able to share the things he had been thinking about for three months.

"Minsu, that was really valuable. We're going to try applying this in our team."

It was Sujin. She meant it.

Minsu smiled. The gremlin's predictions had been wrong.

[.concept-box]
====
*Core Insight*

You do not need to defeat the gremlin.

You only need to notice that it is there.

Awareness alone opens the space between automatic reaction and conscious choice.

That space is your freedom.
====
